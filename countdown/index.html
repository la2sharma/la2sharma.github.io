Why I am counting

Toronto is home now. It gave me a career, a routine, and winters that teach you patience. But some hungers do not translate. You cannot DoorDash the smell of a tandoor at 7 in the morning, or the sound of a steel plate landing on a wooden table with butter melting into every crack of a kulcha.

So I booked the flight. Toronto to Doha, Doha to Amritsar, wheels down in the dark at 2:30 in the morning. I will probably be the most awake person on that plane, because every hour closer is one hour closer to home.

This page is my countdown, and my promise to myself: the first morning back, no meetings, no plans, no sensible schedule. Just the city, the food, and the people I miss.

The first bites

The list is short on purpose. These are the ones I think about when Toronto gets quiet.

01 / THE FIRST BITE

Amritsari Kulcha

Stuffed, crisped in the tandoor, drowned in white butter, with chole on the side. This is the whole reason for the trip. Everything else is a bonus.

Morning one
02 / THE CLASSIC

Lassi

Thick, cold, sweet, with a spoonful of malai on top. The glass is basically a meal, and I would not have it any other way.

With the kulcha
03 / THE GOLDEN HOUR

Jalebi with Rabri

Hot jalebis straight off the kadhai, folded into cool rabri. Best eaten standing outside the shop, sugar on your fingers, no regrets.

Evening walk
04 / THE HUMBLE ONE

Langar at the Golden Temple

Dal, roti, and kheer served to thousands, sitting shoulder to shoulder on the floor. The simplest meal in the city, and somehow the best.

Sri Harmandir Sahib
05 / THE CRUNCH

Amritsari Fish

Fish fried in a spiced besan batter, crisp outside and soft inside, with onions and green chutney. Lawrence Road knows what it is doing.

Lunch run
06 / THE LATE NIGHT

Chicken, Amritsari Style

Tandoori chicken the way only Amritsar makes it, smoky and unapologetic. The city does not sleep early, and neither will I.

After dark
